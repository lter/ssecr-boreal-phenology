# Arctic-Boreal Phenology

## 📌 Project

This project is part of the **[Synthesis Skills for Early Career Researchers (SSECR)](https://lter.github.io/ssecr/)**, organized by the **[Long Term Ecological Research (LTER) Network](https://lternet.edu/)**, running from September to December 2026.

## 🌎 About the project

The group investigates how global warming has changed the **timing and length of seasons** across Arctic-Boreal regions, and how these changes may affect **greenhouse gas (GHG) fluxes**.

Our central question is:

> **How has global warming affected the timing and length of seasons, and the resulting greenhouse gas fluxes in Arctic-Boreal regions?**

The Arctic is warming four to seven times faster than the global average (Rantanen et al., 2022). Changes in the timing of spring, summer, autumn, and winter can affect snow and permafrost thaw, vegetation development, soil conditions, and ultimately carbon cycling and GHG emissions.

We are particularly interested in the **shoulder seasons (spring and autumn)** and their role in shaping ecosystem processes during the following summer and winter.

## 🎯 Objective

A key challenge is to **harmonize observations from different networks and locations**. We will explore ways to combine long-term climate and ecological observations and identify common patterns in seasonal change across the Arctic-Boreal region.

The project may involve:

- Identifying and evaluating relevant long-term datasets.
- Harmonizing variables, temporal coverage, and metadata across datasets.
- Quantifying changes in seasonal timing and duration.
- Linking seasonal changes to ecosystem conditions and GHG fluxes.
- Exploring spatial and temporal patterns across the Arctic-Boreal region.
